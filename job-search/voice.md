# The candidate's voice

<purpose>
Read this before drafting a cover letter, a "why this company" answer, or a resume summary, so the text sounds like the candidate and not like an AI. Add every style correction the candidate makes, with its date, and include it in the execution's PR.
</purpose>

## Defaults (keep, edit, or remove)
- Plain, direct sentences in the first person, the way you'd explain your work to a respected peer.
- Concrete before abstract. Name the product, the number, and the team, then say why it matters.
- Modest about the future. Offer ideas as possibilities ("One possibility is...", "It might help to..."), never "I will transform...".
- No gap lines. Don't name a missing skill, tool, or domain in a letter or form answer unless the question asks.
- No colon setups, "where X meets Y", or lists of three abstract nouns. No balanced split sentences like "X did this, and Y did that".
- Don't spell out well-known acronyms (API, SDK, ML, LLM).
- In a cover letter's value paragraph, describe what the company needs and how you could help without naming past employers.
- Frame what you bring as abilities tied to the company's needs, never as "X taught me".
- Applications show you in the best light. No losses, misses, or mistakes unless the question asks.
- Form answers are 2–3 plain sentences that answer only the question, with no achievements unless it asks for examples.

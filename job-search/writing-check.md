# Writing check

<purpose>
Every resume, cover letter, and "why" answer passes this check before the candidate sees it. It is the full `humanize-writing` skill (`job-search/skills/humanize-writing/SKILL.md`) with the candidate's resume and cover-letter rules added, so this one file is all you need. Recruiters and hiring managers drop text that sounds machine-written, and every claim may come up in an interview. Where this file and `voice.md` differ from the general skill, they win.
</purpose>

<process>
1. **Ground it.** Read `voice.md`. Know the reader (recruiter, ATS, hiring manager) and the one point the piece makes for this company.
2. **Collect the specifics before drafting.** Names, numbers, products, teams, a real moment or opinion. Take them only from `master-resume.md`, `facts.md`, `personal-stories.md`, the company research, or the candidate. Where a detail belongs and you don't have one, leave a visible placeholder like [number] or [YOUR STORY: ...]. Never invent.
3. **Draft once**, following "Write like this" below.
4. **Detect.** Go through "The issues" from top to bottom and list every instance with its number and the exact text. Don't flag company names, titles, dates, degrees, or tool names.
5. **Fix.** Make the smallest change that removes each issue. Keep the candidate's phrasing wherever it works.
6. **Read it aloud (mentally).** Fix anything the candidate wouldn't say to a respected peer, and any stretch where the rhythm goes flat.
7. **Verify facts.** Every employer, title, date, number, and degree must be identical before and after. A fix that changes or adds a fact is reverted.
8. **Repeat** until clean, up to 3 passes. Report anything you couldn't resolve; never accept it silently.
</process>

## Write like this
- **Specific over general.** Name the product, the number, the team. Each paragraph or bullet carries at least one detail only the candidate could know.
- **Plain words.** Use the short, common word (use, help, show, led, built). Say what made something good instead of calling it good.
- **Uneven rhythm.** Mix short sentences with long ones. If three sentences in a row are about the same length, change one. Paragraphs can differ in size.
- **A visible author.** In letters and "why" answers, use "I" where the candidate noticed, decided, or believes something. Hedge only where real doubt exists, once, and keep their modest tone from `voice.md`.
- **Active voice, real verbs.** Pick verbs that state the actual cause and effect, and check the direction is right.
- **Same name for the same thing.** Repeat the key noun rather than cycling synonyms.
- **Start with the point, stop when done.** Open with the claim or the story, not a scene-setter. End on a concrete next step or the last real point, not a summary.
- **Prose in letters.** No bullets, headers, or bold in a cover letter or "why" answer. The resume keeps the format in `resume-build-rules.md`.
- **Ordinary punctuation.** Periods and commas carry most of the load. Use parentheses for asides.

## The issues, in priority order
Fix every instance. Higher items are the most recognizable.

1. **Em dashes, semicolons, and setup colons.** No em dashes (—), no semicolons, and no colon that introduces a list or a setup ("two kinds of work: X and Y", "The result: ...", "Here's the thing: ..."). Use a comma, parentheses, or two sentences. Date ranges take an en dash with spaces (–). Degree names copied verbatim from the master are the only exception.
2. **Contrast and reveal formulas.** "Not just X, but Y", "It's not X. It's Y.", "No X. No Y. Just Z.", "X isn't about Y, it's about Z", "where X meets Y", "Here's why that matters", "The result?", "And honestly?", "That's the real X", "Then I realized". Say the claim directly.
3. **Announce-then-list.** A sentence that says something unites or draws on several things, then lists them instead of making the point: "This role brings together my experience in operations, AI, and change management", "My background combines X, Y, and Z", "It sits at the intersection of A and B". Make the claim directly with one concrete example.
4. **Rule of three.** Three adjectives or three abstract nouns that sound balanced but add nothing. Keep the one that matters, or give the real number of items.
5. **AI words, in any form.** Replace each with the plain verb or the specific thing:
   spearheaded, orchestrated, championed (use led, ran, built) · leverage, utilize (use) · robust, seamless, holistic, comprehensive (say what makes it so) · dynamic, innovative, cutting-edge, transformative, pivotal, crucial, vital, game-changer (cut or use a number) · empower, elevate, foster, unlock, harness (say the result) · delve, navigate or journey used figuratively, landscape, realm, tapestry, testament, beacon, underscore, highlight (as a verb for importance), showcase, boasts, resonate, multifaceted, intricate, nuanced, meticulous, invaluable, vibrant, nestled, quietly, grounded, synergy · passionate, results-driven, detail-oriented, self-starter, thought leader, ecosystem (outside tech).
6. **AI phrases and puffery.** Replace with a concrete claim: "proven track record of", "results-driven professional", "seeking a challenging role", "thrives in a fast-paced environment", "responsible for", "duties included", "hit the ground running", "wear many hats", "drove impactful results", "passionate about leveraging", "a testament to", "when it comes to", "stands as a", "plays a vital role", "serves as a reminder", "marks a pivotal moment", "in today's fast-paced world", "in the ever-evolving landscape".
7. **Empty -ing tails.** Sentences ending in "..., highlighting its importance", "..., reflecting a commitment to", "..., ensuring that", "..., contributing to". Cut the tail or make it a real sentence with a fact.
8. **Vague authority.** "Experts say", "studies show", "many believe", "it is widely regarded". Name the source or drop the claim.
9. **Glue transitions and filler.** Moreover, furthermore, additionally, in addition, that said, ultimately, overall, in conclusion, repeated sentence-opening "however", and repeated "also". Cut "it's important to note that", "it's worth mentioning", "in order to", "the fact that", "very", "really", "truly", "just", and stacked adverbs.
10. **Balanced hedging and false neutrality.** "While X has challenges, it also offers opportunities", "both sides have merit", "despite its challenges". Pick a view or state the specific tradeoff.
11. **Chatbot residue and formatting tics.** "I hope this helps", "Certainly!", offers of further help, notes about the text, Title Case Headings, bold sprinkled on phrases, emoji, curly and straight quotes mixed. Delete, and match the destination's normal format.
12. **Monotone structure.** In prose, every paragraph the same length, each opening with a topic sentence and closing with a recap, or three sentences under 12 words in a row. In the resume, bullets with the same shape, length, and opening verb. Vary them and cut repeats.
13. **Vague bullets.** Every bullet needs a tool, a scope, a number, or an outcome. Take it from `master-resume.md` or `facts.md`; if none exists, ask the candidate or leave a visible placeholder like [number].
14. **Objective-style opening.** The summary opens with who the candidate is and their strongest evidence, not with what they want.
15. **Duty listing.** Rewrite job-description restatements as an action plus a result.
16. **Overselling.** Cut self-praising adjectives, stacked superlatives, and claims bigger than the evidence.
17. **Weak or padded verbs.** Replace "helped to", "assisted with", "worked on", "was involved in", "participated in", "successfully", and vague "various" or "multiple" with exactly what they did. Never upgrade a supporting role to "led".
18. **Voice rules.** Check against `voice.md` and these defaults: modest about the future, no spelled-out well-known acronyms (API, SDK, ML, LLM), no "X taught me", and no past-company name-dropping in the cover letter's value paragraph.

## Never
- Add deliberate typos or fake slang to seem human.
- Chase AI-detector scores. The test is a hiring manager who will meet the candidate.
- Change what the text claims while fixing its style.

## Report to the candidate
End with one line: "Writing check: clean" or "Writing check: fixed N issues; open: <list>".

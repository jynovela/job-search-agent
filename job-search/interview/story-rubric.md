# Story rubric

<purpose>
How to score a story on two scales: **Level** (is it pitched at the right seniority for the role?) and **Content** (is it a good answer?). The `story-bank` and `mock-interview` skills use it. Score each criterion 1–5, where 5 means a hiring manager at the target level would fight for them on this alone. Keep the bar high: a 3 is "fine but forgettable", and fine stories lose senior loops, because the most common reason for a no-hire at senior levels is choosing the wrong story, not structure [interviewing.io].
</purpose>

## Scale 1: Level fit
Score against the role's level, taken from the req, or the target level in `config.md` when the req doesn't say.

| Criterion | What a 5 looks like | Common miss |
|---|---|---|
| **Altitude** | The candidate is clearly the protagonist driving it, and they name the collaborators and stakeholders they influenced | Too low: they're buried in the details with no stakeholders. Too high: it's "we" the whole time |
| **Judgment over mechanics** | The story is about how they broke down an ambiguous problem, weighed options, and chose despite the risk | It lists steps, tools, and tasks they were assigned |
| **Scope** | The impact touches multiple teams or the whole company, and scope is quantified (team size, budget, timeline, core metric) [interviewing.io] | The impact stays local to them or their own team |
| **Trade-offs** | They name the alternative they rejected and why, and can defend the call under challenge [interviewing.io] | "And then the project succeeded." |
| **Leverage** | Director and above: people developed, or a system or process that outlasted them [Lara Hogan] | Their personal output is the whole result |
| **Differentiation** | It shows something beyond what peers at their level routinely do [interviewing.io] | Routine work for the level |

Level expectations: Senior/Lead stories prove ownership, reducing ambiguity, trade-offs, and influence without authority. Director stories prove scaling systems, people development, org change, and strategic resource allocation.

## Scale 2: Content
| Criterion | What a 5 looks like | Common miss |
|---|---|---|
| **Answers the real prompt** | It answers the question behind the question: a teamwork question is really about credit and disagreement | It answers the literal words only |
| **Pacing** | Setup takes 15–30 s, about 60% is action and decisions, and it ends with the result and the lesson [MIT CAPD; the candidate's notes] | Two minutes of background, 30 seconds of action |
| **Tension** | A real dilemma, constraint, difficult stakeholder, or failure | A smooth plan-execute-succeed arc, which reads as fake |
| **Data as supporting actor** | One or two numbers anchor the win, and decisions lead | Reciting a spreadsheet, or no numbers at all |
| **One-line impact** | The interviewer can summarize their impact in one sentence | A wall of plot points |
| **Length** | Under 2 minutes spoken, about 250–300 words [ampd careers; the candidate's notes] | A five-minute saga (split it [RocketBlocks]) |
| **Fit to the req** | It shows one of the req's top 3–5 skills, at the company's scale (scrappy startup vs. enterprise stakeholders) [MIT CAPD; the candidate's notes] | A generic story |
| **Truth** | Every fact matches `master-resume.md` and `facts.md`, and they can go deep on any detail [project rules; r/ExperiencedDevs] | An inflated scope or claim |

## Quick diagnostic
Run these before scoring [interviewing.io]:
1. Can their impact be said in one sentence?
2. Does they own an ambiguous problem, not wait for instructions?
3. Is scope quantified?
4. Can they say why they rejected the alternative?
5. If it's a failure story, is it a real lesson, not a disguised strength?
6. Does they come across as the hero without sounding arrogant?

## How to give feedback
- Name the **lowest-scoring criterion first**, because that's what a hiring manager remembers.
- Give 2–3 concrete fixes as rewritten lines or questions to answer ("Cut the setup to: '…'", "Who pushed back, and what did you say?"), not general advice.
- Say plainly when a story is the wrong story for the bucket or level, and suggest a better candidate from the bank or resume.
